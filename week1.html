The Good, Bad and Funny

A Funny Day in the Park - Serhii

When I was 16 years old, I and a girl were sitting in the local park having fun and talking about the future. It was a sunny day and nothing could go wrong but unfortunately our picnic was interrupted.

While we were discussing our topic, a homeless and drunk man appeared unexpectedly. For a second I thought that he would start arguing with us, but it turned out in a different way. Since he was drunk, he just walked to us and started talking “Hey youth, I have never seen so beautiful couple in my life, you are so handsome and you, lady, you are so glorious.” So we thought that he is just drunk and he want just to say some nice words about us but we were in wrong.

After 5 minutes of flattering us how beautiful we are he finally said what he wanted to say the whole time “So, since you are such a great couple, can you give me some money?” This experience showed me that in Ukraine, even drunk people can come up with so tricky and funny ways to ask for help.

The Morning It Snowed Again - Kourosh

In the city where I was born, heavy snowfalls used to occur every day during the winter months for decades. However, nowadays, it only snows on a few specific days each winter.

Last year, there were many rumors suggesting that we might never see snowfalls again like we used to. The uncertainty and inaccuracy of weather reports made us almost believe it.

But one morning, I woke up to a wonderful surprise: a thick layer of snow covered the entire ground, and the snowfall was intense—just like in the past. It felt like nature was saying, "Yes, there will still be snowfalls in winter." I think that moment has become one of my favorite memories so far.

A Ride Through Moldova - Constantin

For me, the bad part is more of a personal thought. I often wish Moldova was more developed and progressive. A simple example is public transport—there are no trains, only buses, and they’re often slow, overcrowded, and nobody seems to care about fixing it. Sometimes it feels like those funny videos from China during peak hours, where people squeeze in with no space left, except here it’s not really funny when you’re the one stuck inside. Note for Outsiders:

• Overcrowded buses – There are so many people packed into buses that you can barely move or even breathe comfortably, and it is something completely normal to the locals.

When Milk Isn’t Milk - Leandro

My first grocery shopping experience in the Netherlands turned into an impromptu comedy. I had a quick check list of items to buy at the supermarket: cheese, bread, and milk. Simple enough, right? When I got to the dairy aisle, I saw milk in a carton with the word "karnemelk" on it and thought to myself, "That must be the normal milk."

I poured myself a big glass of milk at home, excited to drink something familiar, and, oh boy was I surprised! My first sip was definitely not what I was expecting, it was sour. I quickly thought I had purchased sour milk and was spitting it out, but instead I just started laughing at myself. Buying a bad carton of milk in a new country seemed like a small mistake, but it was so "me".

I check the labels two times now every time I walk down the dairy aisle. What started as a mistake turned into a memory I will probably never forget.

Cultural Insight: Whenever you are living abroad even very mundane things you do every day transform into cultural learning opportunities. Buttermilk (karnemelk) is a staple food in the Netherlands, so it was surprising to discover buttermilk.

karnemelk: Karnemelk is the Dutch word for buttermilk, which is a sour, fermented dairy drink. A lot of people in the Netherlands enjoy it, but it can be confusing to people who are new in the country and expect fresh milk, like me.

Discovering Switzerland’s Clean Side - Niels

When I took my girlfriend, who is Dutch, to Switzerland for the first time, we did the journey by train.

The whole journey was a bit of a debacle, because we had to travel through Germany.

Anyways, once we made it to Switzerland, we had to change trains to a Swiss one.

Once we stepped into the train, I did the normal thing of looking for a place to sit, when my girlfriend asks me, “Should we not move to the second class?”

I was very confused and asked her why she thought she was in the first class, and she responded that it was too clean where we were.

That is when I learned that Switzerland is a very clean land, and that it is not to be taken as a given.
